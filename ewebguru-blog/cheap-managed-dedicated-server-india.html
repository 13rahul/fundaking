Cheap Managed Dedicated Server in India: What I’d Buy After Pricing the Carts

I sat down with the brands people actually name and compared the carts, not the ads. Prices below are public pages from September 2026. Check checkout before you pay.

Ashok Arora · Founder & CEO, eWebGuru · 15 September 2026

I still get the same WhatsApp every other week. Someone has outgrown shared hosting. Their developer said “get a dedicated.” Their accountant said “keep it cheap.” And they want it managed, because nobody on their team wants to patch a kernel at 1 a.m.

That search, cheap managed dedicated server in India, is a mess. The ads mix VPS, self-managed iron, and actual managed boxes as if they were the same product. I got tired of explaining the difference on calls, so I opened the plan pages myself and priced what I would put a client on.

I am going to be biased, because I sell one of these. I am also going to tell you where our card is weak, because you will see it anyway.

I only compared brands that still sell this product

If you typed BigRock, MilesWeb, Hostinger, GoDaddy, OVH or Hetzner into Google last month, I know why. Those names show up. I still refused to line them up as if they sold the same thing.

Hostinger’s India cards are VPS. GoDaddy retired dedicated and moved people onto VPS. OVH Mumbai dedicated is a proper machine, but you are the sysadmin unless you buy their vSphere pack, which starts north of ₹1.5 lakh a month. Hetzner’s “general purpose” cloud is dedicated vCPU IaaS in Europe, the US or Singapore. There is no India region, and you patch it yourself.

Useful products. Wrong article. I wanted a box (or a reserved cloud server) that someone else would mind, in an Indian city, at a price a growing company can pay without a board meeting.

That left four pages on my desk: MilesWeb’s dedicated line, BigRock’s fully managed dedicated, ResellerClub India’s managed dedicated, and our own dedicated cloud servers.

If you only remember one thing from that table, remember this: the cheap headline and the managed headline are not always the same line item.

MilesWeb is the hardware temptation

I will start with MilesWeb because that is the card that makes a technical person sit up. Six cores, twelve threads, 32 GB, 480 GB NVMe, for ₹4,999 on the way in. On paper that beats our starter on every spec except the management story.

I have watched enough carts to know what happens next. You click buy because the NVMe looks serious. Then checkout asks which management tier you want. Self-managed. 24×7. Proactive. The public page does not print that add-on in rupees, which I find annoying when I am trying to advise someone. Backups sit on a “nominal extra” line. Their money-back window is seven days.

If you already have a sysadmin, or you are happy ticking 24×7 and paying whatever they add, DS 6C is a strong machine for the money. I would not tell you it is fake dedicated. I would tell you to screenshot the cart with management on, and to ask where the backups land.

One more irritant: I have seen their Mumbai landing show DS 6C at a different promo. If your sales screenshot says ₹4,999 and mine says something else, neither of us is lying. The cart you paid is the only number that counts.

BigRock is the managed dedicated most Indian buyers already trust

BigRock’s Mach-1 SSD is the clean like-for-like against a 16 GB managed box. Octa core at 2.20 GHz (they do not print the generation on the card, which I wish they would), 16 GB RAM, 500 GB SSD in RAID 1, 5 TB, two IPs, free migration. ₹8,249 a month. Fully managed on that page. India or US.

RAID 1 is the reason a careful buyer pays this. If one disk dies, you are still up. Two IPs help if you want mail and web split, or a spare for SSL gymnastics. That is real value. It is also roughly double our Cloud Server 1, and about ₹2,250 more than our Cloud Server 3, which is the 16 GB / 500 GB plan I would actually put next to Mach-1.

Would I buy Mach-1 for a client who already lives in BigRock for domains and shared hosting? Maybe, if they want one vendor and they want RAID 1 on the invoice. Would I tell a new buyer this is the cheap option? No. You are paying brand and RAID, not a bargain.

ResellerClub looks like BigRock’s cousin, because it is

ResellerClub and BigRock sit in the same group. That does not mean the SKUs match. I opened their India managed dedicated page and the default location was the US. Standard there was ₹13,798 a month on a one-year display. Flip the flag to India and Standard drops to ₹11,998, renewing at ₹14,998.

The India Standard box is 4 vCPU, 32 GB, 500 GB NVMe, unmetered, two IPs. Management copy is on the card: setup, OS hardening, proactive monitoring, 24×7 advanced support. Then you scroll into configure and Managed Services is still sitting there at ₹5,999 a month. The FAQ says managed is an add-on, Linux dedicated only. cPanel is a paid cloud licence. Acronis is extra.

I cannot honestly tell you ₹11,998 is the all-in managed price until someone screenshots a completed cart. What I can tell you is this: they do not have a 16 GB card on that page. Their floor is 32 GB, and even the promo year rate is three times our 32 GB starter. If a reseller client is already billing through ResellerClub, fine. If you are shopping India managed dedicated on price, this is not the cheap aisle.

Month to month on India Standard was ₹13,998. People forget to look at that when the year badge is doing the talking.

What I sell, and the bit I will not hide

Our dedicated cloud is not a single chassis with your name on the front. It is a reserved virtual server on our VMware cluster in Mumbai and Delhi NCR, on our own network, AS133643. CPU and RAM are not oversold. That is the whole point of the product. You get root, a dedicated IPv4, 1-click RAM/CPU/disk, weekly off-server backups, free migration with no site limit, Sophos in front, and a 30-day money-back window. Windows Server is ₹1,500 extra.

Cloud Server 1 is ₹3,995: 4 cores, 16 GB, 100 GB SSD, 5 TB. That 100 GB is the weak spec. I would rather you hear it from me than from a screenshot in a WhatsApp group. If your sites, mail and backups will not fit, do not squeeze them. Cloud Server 2 is 200 GB at ₹4,495. Cloud Server 3 is 500 GB at ₹5,995. Extra SSD is ₹500 per 100 GB if you want to stay on the small plan and grow disk only.

For 32 GB, I would not pretend Cloud Server 1 is the comparison. Cloud Server 5 is 4 cores, 32 GB, 200 GB, ₹5,495. Cloud Server 6 is 6 cores, 32 GB, 500 GB, ₹7,245. That is the honest stack against MilesWeb DS 6C and ResellerClub Standard.

What you lose versus a physical dedicated card: you are not buying a whole machine. You cannot sit in RAID BIOS. What you gain is the thing I actually use on our own fleet. If a node gets sick, the workload is not welded to one failed disk cage. If you need 8 GB more RAM next month because a sale weekend got stupid, we resize. I cannot do that on a chassis without a truck roll or a migration.

Managed means something at 1 a.m., or it means a badge

I have lost count of “managed” invoices where the customer still SSH’d in to install a panel, and we still got the panic call when PHP-FPM filled the disk with logs. So this is how I read the four pages.

On our cloud dedicated, management is in the monthly. We patch. We monitor. Backups run off the server every week. You still have root, so you can break things, and I would like you not to. That tension is normal on a managed box with root. We are not taking the keys away. We are catching the boring failures.

On MilesWeb, the machine is yours either way. The question is whether you paid for the humans. If you leave it on self-managed to keep the ₹4,999, you do not get to be surprised when a failed yum update is your Friday night.

On BigRock’s managed dedicated page, the Mach-1 price is the managed price. That is cleaner. I still ask what their backup product is, because RAID 1 is not a backup. RAID 1 is two disks agreeing. If you delete a database, they both delete it.

On ResellerClub, I would not argue with their marketing copy. I would argue with the configure widget. If you buy this, make the salesperson put “managed included, or ₹5,999 extra” in writing.

If I were buying for your workload next week

I would not start from brand. I would start from RAM, disk, and whether you have a human who can own the OS.

If you need about 16 GB, you want India, and you want someone else watching the box, I would put you on our Cloud Server 1 or 3. One if your sites are modest. Three if you have mail, staging, and a few WordPress installs that hoard uploads. BigRock Mach-1 if RAID 1 and two IPs are the hill you want to die on, and ₹8,249 does not bother you.

If you need 32 GB and you care more about cores and NVMe than about included management, I would price MilesWeb DS 6C with 24×7 or proactive actually ticked, then put our Cloud Server 5 or 6 next to it. Their disk and thread count will look better. Our backups, 30-day window, named cities, and resize will look better. Pick the boring one that matches who is going to get the 2 a.m. call.

If a reseller panel is already how you bill customers, ResellerClub Standard on the India toggle is a product. It is not a cheap one. I would not cross-shop it against ₹3,995 unless you like disappointing yourself.

I would not buy Hostinger or GoDaddy for this job. I would not buy OVH Mumbai unless you want to be the sysadmin and you have already budgeted the setup fee. I would not buy Hetzner cloud for an India audience that needs in-country hosting.

The mistake I see on these quotes

People compare intro prices and then act shocked at renewal. MilesWeb’s DS 6C renews at ₹5,499. ResellerClub’s India Standard renews at ₹14,998 after a year badge of ₹11,998. Our ₹3,995 is the list on the plan page, not a 24-month teaser. If your CFO only reads the big green number, you will have an awkward month 13.

The other mistake is buying 32 GB because 16 GB felt “small,” then filling 20 GB with staging copies and local backups. Disk is cheaper than RAM. Put backups off the machine. That is why we include weekly off-server copies. I still see people dump tar.gz onto the same SSD they run MySQL on, then tell me the server is slow.

Last one: treating dedicated cloud and dedicated metal as insults or trophies. They are tools. If you need a GPU or a very specific RAID layout, you want metal. If you need reserved cores in India and you will grow RAM twice this year, you want the cloud dedicated line. I run both. I do not pretend they are the same box.

What I would do if it were my company

If this were my own store, my own agency sites, my own staging, I would start on our dedicated cloud at ₹3,995 or ₹5,995 depending on disk, take the 30 days seriously, and watch RAM and disk for a month. I would not jump to ResellerClub’s 32 GB floor to feel enterprise. I would not take MilesWeb’s NVMe without paying for management unless I still enjoyed being on-call. I would not pay BigRock ₹8,249 unless RAID 1 was a requirement someone wrote down, not a feeling.

That is a cheaper managed dedicated server in India as I actually buy it. Not the loudest spec sheet. The cart I can defend to you after something breaks.

If you want us to spec it, tell me RAM, how much disk you really use today, Linux or Windows, and whether you need RAID 1. I will tell you Cloud Server 1, 3, 5 or 6, or I will tell you to buy MilesWeb or BigRock instead. I have done that on calls. I would rather lose a wrong deal than run a 100 GB disk under a 400 GB website and pretend I did not see it coming.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest managed dedicated server in India right now?

On the public pages I used in September 2026, our dedicated cloud starts at ₹3,995 with management included. MilesWeb DS 6C is ₹4,999 on intro, but that is the hardware. Management is extra at checkout. Always confirm the cart. These pages change promo badges without warning me.

Is dedicated cloud the same as a dedicated server?

No. Dedicated cloud, the way we sell it, is reserved CPU and RAM on a cluster. Not oversold. A dedicated server is usually the whole physical machine. Both can be managed. I pick cloud dedicated when I want to resize. I pick metal when the workload is welded to one chassis on purpose.

Why didn’t you include Hostinger?

They sell VPS in India. This piece is managed dedicated and dedicated cloud. Putting a ₹599 VPS next to these cards would make the VPS look cheap and the dedicated look stupid. That is a different article.

MilesWeb has more RAM and NVMe. Why would I pay you?

If the DS 6C cart has 24×7 or proactive management and paid backups, and you want that EPYC box, I would not talk you out of it. If you want management, weekly off-server backups, a 30-day window, Mumbai or Delhi NCR on our ASN, and 1-click resize, that is the gap. Compare Cloud Server 5 or 6, not Cloud Server 1, when they are waving 32 GB at you.

Is BigRock Mach-1 worth ₹8,249?

It is if RAID 1 and two IPs are what you came for, and you already like BigRock. Against our Cloud Server 3 at ₹5,995 you are paying a few thousand extra for that RAID story and the brand. CPU generation is not on their card. Ask them before you assume it is new silicon.

Should I use ResellerClub or BigRock?

Same group, different SKUs. Mach-1 SSD is the 16 GB managed dedicated. ResellerClub India’s Standard is a 32 GB NVMe card at a much higher rupee, and you have to switch the location to India. I would pick based on the panel you already bill through, not because one homepage felt more premium.
