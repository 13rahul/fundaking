Firing an SEO agency is a business decision, not a tantrum about rankings. Use this scorecard when organic pipeline stalls while the monthly PDF still looks green.

## Hard fire signals (any two = serious conversation)

1. No access to the strategist who makes decisions — only account managers reading slides  
2. Cannot produce a five-query battleground sheet with screenshots  
3. Money URLs still have crawl/index defects after two quarters  
4. Reporting is rankings-only; demos/calls never appear  
5. Content volume without cluster architecture or internal links to BOFU  
6. Contradictory facts remain on pricing/About/GBP after you flagged them  

## Soft fire signals (coach first, then fire)

- Slow ticket turnaround but honest communication  
- Good technical work, weak commercial prioritisation  
- Over-indexed on one channel (only blogs, or only links)

## The 30-day exit / transition plan

**Week 1:** Export all logins, GSC, analytics, content inventory, backlink exports.  
**Week 2:** Freeze new vanity content; list P0 technical defects.  
**Week 3:** Decide next model — in-house, consultant, or hybrid (see [in-house vs consultant vs agency](/blog/in-house-seo-vs-consultant-vs-agency)).  
**Week 4:** Hand off roadmap; rewrite one money URL so momentum does not die.

Do not ghost the agency without securing credentials. Do not hire a replacement on “guaranteed #1” pitches.

## What “good” looks like after the switch

A 90-day roadmap tied to revenue URLs, weekly KPI tiles founders read, and a strategist who stays after the audit. That is how [SEO consulting](/seo-consulting-services) is scoped from Pune / Baramati — founder-led, capped engagements.

## FAQ

**Should we fire mid-contract?**  
Read the SOW. Often a scoped wind-down beats paying for theatre.

**What if rankings are up but leads are flat?**  
That is still a fail — fire the KPI system even if you keep some tactics.

**Is a cheaper freelancer the answer?**  
Only with a clear roadmap and QA. Cheap without prioritisation repeats the problem.

**How do we evaluate the next partner?**  
Use the [SEO RFP template](/blog/seo-rfp-template-india) and demand case-level proof.

**Can we pause SEO entirely for a quarter?**  
You can pause publishing; do not pause money-URL monitoring.

## Conversation script with the agency

Bring: five-query sheet, money-URL crawl defects, pipeline trend, and two examples of ranking-only reporting. Ask for a written recovery plan in seven days with owners and dates. If they respond with more keyword promises, you have your answer.

## Legal and operational offboarding checklist

Credentials, GSC ownership verification, ad platform access if bundled, content file exports, backlink vendor contracts, outstanding invoices, non-disparagement realities. Assign an internal owner before the final call.

## Interim 60-day DIY mode

Freeze new links purchases. Fix P0 technical. Rewrite one BOFU page. Keep publishing only if it supports that page. Hire next partner against a written RFP, not a LinkedIn DM.

## Leadership decisions around “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)”

### Solo marketer path

You have ten hours a week. For “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)”, pick one revenue URL this topic supports, write acceptance criteria for engineering (status code, visible copy, schema match), and ship a single scannable asset sales can forward after a call. Refuse new blog ideas until that URL is green in Search Console and linked from your cluster hub.

Track work in a Sheet with columns: URL, owner, due date, status, KPI (demos, calls, WhatsApp, or qualified pipeline). Review it in the pipeline meeting — not a separate SEO theatre call.

### Already-have-an-agency path

Use this article as a scorecard. Ask for evidence that the checks here are done on your money URLs. If the agency only returns keyword greenery, escalate once with a written 14-day recovery plan. If they cannot speak to the substance of “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)”, change the operating model.

### Scaling-content path

Volume is not the strategy. Gate every new outline on: which prompt or query it supports, which URL owns it, and which demo stage it influences. “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)” should appear in the quarterly roadmap with a named owner and a kill criterion if pipeline does not move.

## Commercial QA gates for “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)”

**Discovery**
- [ ] Five buyer prompts or queries documented from sales or support
- [ ] Primary URL chosen; competing URLs listed for consolidation
- [ ] Screenshot baseline of SERP / Maps / AI answer dated

**Execution**
- [ ] Technical gates on the primary URL pass (200, indexable, facts in HTML)
- [ ] First screen rewritten in buyer language, not internal jargon
- [ ] At least one table, process list, or FAQ block that can be quoted
- [ ] Hub ↔ spoke ↔ service ↔ proof links with descriptive anchors

**Proof and QA**
- [ ] Claims with numbers are dated or scoped to India / segment
- [ ] Organization and author facts match About and schema
- [ ] Sales reviewed the page and would send it to a prospect

**Measurement**
- [ ] KPI tile exists on the founder dashboard
- [ ] Biweekly or weekly review assigned
- [ ] Decision logged after 30 days: double down, fix, or cut

## Pushbacks I hear on “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)”

**“We need more content first.”**  
Not if money URLs fail crawl or contradict pricing. Volume without architecture dilutes topical authority and wastes writers.

**“Our agency said rankings are up.”**  
Ask for demos and the five-query sheet. Rankings without pipeline are a vanity report.

**“AI will handle this automatically.”**  
Models amplify whatever facts you publish. Wrong NAP, wrong pricing, and JS-only answers get amplified too.

**“We are too early for SEO.”**  
You are not too early for clean money URLs and entity facts. You may be too early for a large content team — that is different.

## Calendar — first 45 days on “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)”

**Days 1–5:** Baseline data, choose primary URL, list P0 defects.  
**Days 6–15:** Ship technical and factual fixes; rewrite lede + FAQ.  
**Days 16–30:** Wire internal links; update service CTA; brief sales.  
**Days 31–45:** Measure; screenshot again; decide next URL. Do not start URL #2 until URL #1 is honestly Pass on technical + entity checks.

## FAQ — implementation details for “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)”

**Does this replace the cluster hub article?**  
No — this spoke implements one job; the hub frames the pillar.

**How do we keep pages from going stale?**  
Revisit when pricing, packaging, or service area changes. Stale BOFU claims are worse than thin TOFU posts.

**What is the minimum evidence for case-study claims?**  
Named process and scoped outcomes you can defend on a sales call — not invented percentages.

**Should every blog have the same CTA?**  
No. Match intent: Maps/WhatsApp for local, demo for SaaS, audit call for consulting.

**When is “When to Fire Your SEO Agency (And What to Do Next)” not P0?**  
When crawl/index on revenue templates is broken, or when ops cannot handle leads. Fix those constraints first.
