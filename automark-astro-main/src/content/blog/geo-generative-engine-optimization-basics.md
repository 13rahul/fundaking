When “GEO” started trending, my inbox filled with the same question: “Is this different from SEO?” **The tactics overlap; the measurement does not.** Generative Engine Optimization, the way I use the term with clients, means improving how often and how accurately generative systems represent your brand when they answer real buyer prompts.

I do not sell GEO as magic. I sell a **repeatable operating system**—because one audit deck does nothing if sales still hears wrong pricing from ChatGPT three months later.




## The five parts of a sober GEO program

**1. Prompt library** — I pull 30–50 questions from CRM notes, support tickets, and discovery calls. If the prompt did not come from a human who pays you, it is low priority.

**2. Baseline audit** — Same prompts, two or three engines, log cited URLs, quoted lines, and whether the answer is accurate. This is your before picture.

**3. Content gaps** — Missing comparisons, vague pricing, no implementation timeline, weak “what is” category page. I fix gaps in **format**, not volume.

**4. Off-site consistency** — LinkedIn, G2, Crunchbase, PDFs your team emails—aligned names, pricing rules, integrations.

**5. Monthly re-test** — Citation share, sentiment, factual errors. GEO is a cadence, not a project.

## What I refuse to do

Black-hat “AI bait” paragraphs stuffed with synonyms. Fake statistics. Pages that exist only to catch bots. Models and search systems both learn to deprioritize low-utility fluff—and your brand risk is not worth it.

## GEO vs classic SEO in one table

| Classic SEO | GEO emphasis |
| --- | --- |
| Rankings & CTR | Citation share & accuracy |
| Keywords | Prompts & passages |
| Title tags | Definitional ledes |
| Backlinks | Mentions + proof pages |

You still want both. I just do not pretend rankings alone protect you when the SERP summarizes.

## A real example (anonymized)

A B2B SaaS client lost a deal because Copilot quoted old pricing from a blog comment thread. We did not write 20 new posts. We shipped a **pricing FAQ**, aligned G2 and the site, added schema, and published a comparison page. Six weeks later, the same prompt cited their pricing URL—not a forum.

## Your next step

Build the prompt library before you hire anyone for GEO. Run the baseline yourself in an afternoon. Fix factual errors before you chase thought leadership.

## Why founders should care now

GEO is measured work: prompts, citations, facts, and BOFU pages—not a rebranded SEO retainer with new buzzwords. That is not theory for me — it shows up on discovery calls when organic pipeline stalls or when AI answers describe a competitor instead of your brand. Search and answer engines reward **specific, verifiable pages**, not generic SEO filler.

If you are evaluating priorities, ask whether this topic touches **revenue pages** (pricing, product, comparisons) or **trust pages** (about, case studies, docs). Fix trust and structure before you scale content volume.

## Mistakes I see on live sites

- Chasing tactics from Twitter threads without a baseline audit in Search Console and analytics.
- Publishing more blogs when the root issue is indexation, faceted URLs, or slow LCP on money pages.
- Treating AI visibility as separate from technical SEO — crawlers and models both need clean HTML and consistent facts.
- Skipping internal links from new posts to BOFU pages, so traffic lands nowhere useful.

## A 30-day workflow you can run in-house

**Week 1 — Baseline:** Export top landing pages by impressions, list five prompts or queries buyers actually use, screenshot current AI/search results for your brand.

**Week 2 — Fix facts:** Align pricing, integrations, and founder bio across site, GBP, and LinkedIn. One wrong number in an old blog comment can poison summaries.

**Week 3 — Ship one asset:** Pick a single page tied to “GEO Basics: Generative Engine Optimization Without the Hype” and rewrite the lede, add a comparison table or checklist, improve internal links.

**Week 4 — Measure:** Track qualified leads or demo requests from organic — not rank positions alone. Re-run your prompt library and log citation changes.
